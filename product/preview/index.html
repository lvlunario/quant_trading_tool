04 / Options Lab · synthetic

Premium is not the same as profit

Fixed cash-secured put example: one standard 100-share contract, $50 strike, $2 premium per share, $0 assumed fees. Gross cash obligation: $5,000. Expiration breakeven: $48.

Underlying ends at $0 — severe downside

Premium $200 minus $5,000 intrinsic loss = −$4,800. Assignment requires buying 100 shares at $50 even when they are worthless.

Underlying ends at $48 — breakeven

Premium $200 minus $200 intrinsic loss = $0.

Underlying ends at $60 — limited premium gain

The put expires out of the money. Modeled profit: $200. There is no additional participation in the stock's rise.

Actionable option comparison blocked: no current chain or reconciled collateral. Fixed expiration illustrations omit early assignment, taxes, interest, trading spreads and fees beyond the stated zero assumption. Not an order or a recommendation.
